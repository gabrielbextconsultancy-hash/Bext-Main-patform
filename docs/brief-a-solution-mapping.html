1. What the brief asked for

A single consolidated news sheet, delivered by email at 05:00 AEST every day, covering industry sources across five named categories, so that the consultant stays current without reading sixty websites.

The system is built and running. It is not a proposal — reports have been generated and delivered on 6 and 7 August 2026. The remaining work to 18 August is refinement, not construction.

2. Solution architecture

IngestHourly. RSS, HTTP scrape, or headless browser per source. Deduplicated on content hash.
StorePostgreSQL. Article, source, and analysis tables with full history.
ScoreSelf-hosted Nous Hermes 3. Summary, relevance 0–100, topic tags.
RenderRanked within each briefed category, above a relevance threshold.
Send05:00 Australia/Melbourne, SMTP today, Microsoft Graph once consented.

Every component is self-hosted on infrastructure that transfers to BEXT at handover. There is no per-article cost and no third-party platform holding the data — which is what the companion brief asks for and what keeps the running cost to a single VPS.

Why three ingest methods rather than one

The sixty-eight briefed sources do not behave alike. Testing each one individually established that:

  • 26 publish a usable RSS feed — cheapest and most reliable, and carries real publication dates.
  • 29 need HTML scraping — no feed exists, or the feed is a stub. Eleven government feeds return valid RSS containing zero items, which looks healthy from outside and yields nothing.
  • 15 refuse automated HTTP entirely — including AEMO, DCCEEW, DEECA, VicGrid and SEC Victoria. These sit behind bot protection that answers a plain request with a challenge page. A headless-browser service solves the challenge and reads the page as a browser would.

The browser path exists because those blocked sources are the Australian energy regulators. Dropping them was not an option the brief allows.

Two categories deferred by the brief itself

Grants / Funding Updates and LinkedIn Industry Behaviour are marked in the brief as “link to other report” — a later deliverable. They are represented in the sheet as sections pointing to that separate report, not silently omitted.

LinkedIn needs a scope decision. The brief asks for the feed to be scrolled for all latest posts. LinkedIn's terms prohibit automated scrolling and their API does not expose the feed. Workable alternatives are the Pages API for company content, or a curated source list. This is a platform limit, not an effort limit.

7. Marketing platform health — outstanding

The brief asks that the sheet also report on the health of the marketing platforms alongside the industry news. The platforms are not yet connected, so this section is not yet built. It is the largest single gap remaining against the brief and is scheduled before 18 August.

8. Method

All figures were read directly from the production database on 7 August 2026. Source classification came from testing each of the sixty-eight URLs individually — feed discovery, plain HTTP fetch, then headless browser — rather than assuming behaviour from the site name. Where a source is recorded as unreachable, that reflects an observed HTTP response, and the response code is stored against the source.
