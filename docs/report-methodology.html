2 · How scoring works

Every article is scored 0–100 by relevance to one specific business: an Australian energy-efficiency consultancy working on existing commercial buildings, with Victoria as its main market. The single test is: could this change what BEXT advises a building owner to do, what a project costs, or what the rules require?

The score bands

80–100 — act on this week. Australian regulatory change, Victorian schemes, market rule changes, or funding a client could act on now.
55–79 — solid industry news. Projects, technology, policy direction, credible research with practical bearing.
20–54 — tangential. Real energy content but remote from their work, or overseas news with no Australian read-across.
0–19 — out of scope. Not energy/building/climate, foreign items with no local read-across, site navigation, promotion, obituaries, early-stage lab research.

Solar comes first

Solar and PV are the core of the business, so the scorer ranks on a priority ladder rather than treating all in-scope topics equally:

  • Tier 1 — Solar / PV a commercial owner could act on: rooftop and behind-the-meter systems, batteries paired with solar, feed-in and export rules, connection and inverter standards, panel supply and pricing, accreditation, and the schemes that fund them (Solar Victoria, VEU solar measures, the federal rebate).
  • Tier 2 — Building energy performance: NABERS, Commercial Building Disclosure, the National Construction Code, GEMS, efficiency upgrades, electrification away from gas, heat pumps.
  • Tier 3 — Wider energy market: generation, transmission, network rules, national policy — genuine context, ranked below the two tiers above.

Two deliberate traps

A regulator publishing something is not a regulatory change. An AER or AEMC decision, rule change or forward-looking speech is high value; a Q&A, an explainer, a graduate program or an annual report is not — however central the regulator.

Early-stage laboratory research scores low even when it is about solar. A new cell chemistry or a lab efficiency record changes nothing a consultant advises this year. Research earns 55+ only when a practitioner could act on it now.

3 · How filtering works

The score is the single dial. Three surfaces read it at three different lines, because they serve different purposes.

Dashboard (full record)0Every article fetched, including noiseThe complete audit trail — proves what was caught and what was rejected.

The noise line: why 16

The client's instruction was precise: scores 1 to 15 are noise and not wanted; everything 16 and above belongs in the email. The scorer places genuinely off-topic material at or below 15, so cutting at 16 removes exactly that band while keeping every real article above it — including the weak-but-on-topic 20–39 items. A second guard, report_eligible, separately holds back archive material discovered in bulk (e.g. 2022–2023 articles unlocked by a parser fix) so old stories are never presented as today's news.
