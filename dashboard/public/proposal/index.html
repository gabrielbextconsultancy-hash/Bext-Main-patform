The ask

What the brief asks for

Maximise time spent consulting. Minimise administration.

Stated outcomes

  • Single point of data entry wherever possible
  • Minimal duplication across systems
  • Maximum use of AI for administrative tasks
  • Highly automated document creation
  • Automated meeting documentation
  • Automated project administration, filing and organisation
  • High-quality draft emails and reports needing only review
  • Consistent processes, scalable, secure

The binding constraint

“Confined to the shared capability of the following tools without need for third-party management platform.”

This is the most important line in the brief. It rules out Monday, Notion, Asana, Zapier and every other SaaS layer. The design therefore uses what is already licensed, joined by automation you own and can hand over.

Section 9 still asks for software recommendations — answered, but deliberately short.

Diagnosis

The same fact gets entered four times

A single client conversation currently produces manual work in four places, because nothing carries information forward:

MEETING
Discussion happens notes typed by hand
HUBSPOT
Contact and stage updated re-entered
PROJECTMANAGER
Actions become tasks re-entered
OUTLOOK
Follow-up written written from scratch
SHAREPOINT
Documents filed named and moved by hand

The principle

Capture once. Distribute automatically. Review at the end, not throughout.

The meeting already produces a recording and a transcript. Everything above can be derived from it — which makes the meeting workflow the highest-leverage place to start, and the brief already names it as the top priority.

What this is not

Not autonomous sending. Every outbound artefact — email, proposal, invoice trigger — stops at a draft for approval. The brief asks for “review and refinement and final approval”, and the design keeps that gate everywhere it touches a client.

How it is built

Graph API subscribes to meeting-transcript events. n8n pulls the transcript, runs it through the drafting prompt with your minutes template, then writes to ProjectManager, SharePoint and an Outlook draft.

Extends to daily notes

The brief asks that this cover personal brainstorming too. Same pipeline, different template — a voice note or OneNote page in, structured and filed output back.

Open question

Teams transcription must be enabled tenant-wide, and app-only transcript access needs an application access policy. Confirm licensing before 25 August — this is the one place where the plan depends on a setting outside our control.

Review area 9 · deliverable 5

Software recommendations

The brief prefers adding nothing. Three additions are proposed, all self-hosted or already owned — and one thing is explicitly not recommended.

AdditionJustification CostEffortMaintenance
n8n self-hosted The integration layer. Without it, the platforms cannot talk without a third-party SaaS — which the brief rules out. ~A$12/mo VPSAlready runningLow — you own it
Vector knowledge base self-hosted Lets AI draft from your past work instead of generically. This is what makes section 7 real. IncludedLowLow
AI API Drafting engine. Claude or Gemini; you already use Claude. ~A$30–80/moLowNone
Power BI Desktop already free Business dashboard over HubSpot, ProjectManager and Xero. FreeMediumLow

Not recommended

A dedicated practice management platform. It would duplicate HubSpot, ProjectManager and Xero, cost more than everything above combined, and re-introduce exactly the third-party dependency the brief asks to avoid.

Worth a decision

Microsoft 365 Business Premium over Standard — adds Intune and better security controls for client data. Relevant to the “secure handling of client and business information” outcome. Modest per-seat increase.

Honest assessment

Risks and open questions

Stated now rather than discovered in September.

RiskDetailResponse
Teams transcript access App-only access to meeting transcripts requires the right licensing and an application access policy. If unavailable, the highest-priority workflow needs a different trigger. Confirm before 25 Aug. Fallback: transcript export on meeting end.
LinkedIn feed monitoring The brief asks for scrolling all latest posts. The platform's terms prohibit this and the API does not expose the feed. Rescope to permitted sources, or remove from scope.
Canva automation depth The Canva API supports asset and template operations but not arbitrary layout generation. Fully automated deck build may be partial. Prototype during architecture phase; Office fallback.
AI draft quality Drafts are only as good as the knowledge base behind them. Thin source material means generic output. Seed with real past work early; measure on real tasks.
Single point of failure The automation layer becomes business-critical. Self-hosted with backups; every workflow degrades to the current manual process rather than blocking it.

On scope. Two items in the brief — LinkedIn feed monitoring and full Canva generation — cannot be delivered exactly as written because of platform limits rather than effort. Both are flagged here so the scope can be adjusted deliberately rather than quietly missed.
